## The Idea

Artificial intelligence can produce an impressive answer in seconds. But confidence doesn't guarantee accuracy, and a well-written explanation isn't necessarily a good one.

That's what interests me.

I'm Stefania, a law student and legal research professional exploring how we evaluate the quality, reliability, and usefulness of AI-generated responses.

My background is in questioning arguments, examining evidence, and identifying what doesn't quite add up. I'm interested in bringing that perspective into the world of AI.

This repository is where I'm putting that curiosity into practice.

## What Makes a Good Answer?

I approach AI evaluation through five questions:

| | Evaluation Area | What I'm Looking For |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Accuracy** | Are the claims factually correct and verifiable? |
| 02 | **Instruction Following** | Did the model actually answer the question it was asked? |
| 03 | **Reasoning** | Does the logic hold up, or does it just sound convincing? |
| 04 | **Clarity** | Is the response understandable and well-structured? |
| 05 | **Helpfulness** | Does the answer genuinely meet the user's needs? |

The goal isn't simply to choose the better response. It's to explain *why* it's better, identify what went wrong, and understand what a stronger answer would look like.

## The Process

**01 / The Prompt**

Start with a question that requires more than a surface-level answer.

**02 / The Comparison**

Examine two AI-generated responses to the same prompt.

**03 / The Evaluation**

Assess each response against a consistent set of criteria, paying attention to errors, omissions, and unsupported claims.

**04 / The Verdict**

Choose the stronger response and explain the decision with specific evidence.

**05 / The Reflection**

Consider what both responses could have done better.

## A Different Perspective

I didn't come into AI through computer science. I came into it through research, writing, and law.

And I think that's an interesting place to start.

Legal research teaches you that an argument isn't strong simply because it's persuasive. It needs support. Sources matter. Context matters. The details matter.

I see a similar challenge in AI evaluation: distinguishing between answers that appear reliable and answers that actually are.

That's the perspective I want to develop through this project.
