# The Black Box in the Courtroom
*Independent Legal Research | Ongoing*

### When technology becomes evidence, who decides whether it can be trusted?

Artificial intelligence is changing the way information is produced, interpreted, and presented in legal proceedings. But the introduction of algorithmic evidence raises questions that traditional legal frameworks weren't necessarily designed to answer.

My current research explores the relationship between algorithmic decision-making, evidentiary reliability, and judicial oversight.

### What I'm Investigating

- **The Black Box Problem** — What happens when an algorithm produces a result that cannot be meaningfully examined?
- **Evidence & Admissibility** — How do existing evidentiary standards apply to algorithmic systems?
- **Explainability vs. Reliability** — Does understanding how an algorithm reaches a conclusion make that conclusion trustworthy?
- **The Role of the Court** — How should judges approach the evaluation of technically complex evidence?

### Research Focus

My work examines the application of U.S. evidentiary standards, including *Daubert* and Federal Rule of Evidence 702, to algorithmic evidence.

I'm particularly interested in the tension between technological sophistication and the legal system's responsibility to ensure that evidence is sufficiently reliable.
